# Deep research map: retention decisions, causal evaluation, and product experiments

**Reviewed:** 28 September 2026  
**Status:** literature review and proposed experiments; no new model result is claimed here.  
**Companion:** [project review and roadmap](PROJECT_REVIEW_AND_EXPANSION_2026_EN.md), [existing finite-sample related work](RELATED_WORK.md).

## 1. Research question and evidence boundary

For an eligible customer at a fixed decision time, which feasible action maximizes **incremental retained value after costs**, subject to a contact cap, budget, and customer protections? A churn score estimates an outcome under observed behavior. It cannot by itself answer how the outcome would change if an offer were sent. In two field experiments, [Ascarza (2018)](https://evaascarza.com/papers/ascarza_18.pdf) found that highest-risk customers were not necessarily the best retention targets. This directly motivates the project's risk-versus-response comparison, but it does not imply an uplift learner will win on every dataset.

Use potential outcomes to keep claims precise. Let `A=0` mean no contact, `A=a` a specific offer, and `Y(a)` retained margin within a fixed horizon. For a policy `pi(X)`, the target is `V(pi)=E[Y(pi(X))-C(pi(X),X)]`, with capacity and eligibility constraints. For binary churn, reverse the outcome sign consistently or define prevented churn `Y(0)-Y(1)`; never mix that with retained margin. A model score is a ranking input. A policy is a mapping from features to action. A **policy-value difference** is the primary decision metric.

The current public sources are separate experiments: IBM supports prediction only; Orange and Hillstrom support limited randomized treatment analyses; Lenta is an observational retail SMS analysis; the temporal lab has known simulated counterfactuals. They do not form one telecom intervention panel with real costs, repeat contacts, and observed margin. The planner currently uses explicit assumed economics. Label every chart and export as *measured randomized*, *observational under assumptions*, *simulated known truth*, or *scenario assumption*.

## 2. Literature synthesis, with implications for this repository

| Recent retention comparison: [Ruf & Handrich (2026)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/14707853261423690) | Matching plus meta-learners beat propensity targeting in their evaluation. | Estimation: [Künzel et al. ### A methodological correction worth showcasing

The existing [related-work audit](RELATED_WORK.md) distinguishes **constant but imbalanced randomized assignment** from weak overlap where `e(X)` approaches zero for some covariate regions. The repo's finite-sample coverage failure under constant `e=0.9` is primarily a rare weighted-score/asymmetry problem; it is not evidence that a weak-overlap theorem has been newly discovered here. [Qiu (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18898) studies finite-sample AIPW Wald coverage in randomized trials; [Dorn (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13273) and [Ma et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08974) address weak-overlap regimes. A portfolio claim should be a *tested operating boundary and reproducible diagnostic*, not a new theorem.

Cross-fitting estimates nuisance functions on other folds and can reduce overfitting bias in orthogonal scores; see [Chernozhukov et al. (2018)](https://academic.oup.com/ectj/article/21/1/C1/5056401). It does **not** make a confidence interval valid after the same evaluation outcomes choose the policy, interval variant, or favorable arm. Separate algorithm development, interval validation, and final policy assessment. A deterministic tuning *algorithm* can be frozen even if it picks different hyperparameters in different development folds; instability still affects robustness and the reason to spend a confirmation split.

## 3. What the existing data can identify

| Data | Action/outcome available | Strongest defensible study | Missing for the expanded product |
|---|---|---|---|
| IBM Telco | Churn labels and customer features; no randomized contact. | Risk prediction, calibration, input validation, scoring latency. | Intervention effect, actual time horizon, costs, causal ROI. |
| Orange | Randomized contact indicator and churn, with assignment-integrity concerns in pooled data. | Conditional treatment-policy evaluation inside audited design region; current `NO_LAUNCH`. | Campaign keys, customer time series, cost/margin ledger. |
| Hillstrom | Randomized `No E-Mail`, `Mens E-Mail`, `Womens E-Mail`; visit, conversion, spend outcomes. | Development-only binary and multi-arm policy comparison with known assignment; untouched confirmation remains closed. | Retention/churn endpoint, actual email cost, long-term margin. |
| Lenta | SMS treatment and `response_att`; treatment is observational. | Sensitivity and transfer stress test under stated measured-confounding assumption. | Randomization, hidden-confounder audit, telecom outcome. |
| Synthetic temporal lab | Generated potential outcomes, drift, repeated customer-month records. | True regret, harmful-contact rate, false-promotion and constraint tests. | Field effectiveness. |

Avoid feature leakage: Hillstrom `visit`, `conversion`, and `spend` are post-treatment outcomes; Lenta response-channel fields are not baseline features. Assess positivity **by action and slice**, record actual arm event counts, and use assignment probabilities from design documents where known. In observational Lenta, estimate propensity only on pre-treatment covariates and report balance, effective sample size, overlap, and an unmeasured-confounding sensitivity analysis; balance is diagnostic, not proof of exchangeability.

## 4. Experiment protocols to run next

The designs below are **proposals**, not achieved results. Freeze new parameter grids and acceptance thresholds before running; the cited `0.92` coverage gate is an existing project threshold. Record dataset hash, code commit/archive digest, environment, seeds, feature cutoff, estimand, action probabilities, cost source, splits, candidate recipes, search budget, primary contrast, uncertainty method, and a stop rule in each run manifest. Report negative and failed runs. The existing Hillstrom confirmation split stays closed until the full procedure is registered and the evaluator's selected interval is independently validated.

### E1 — Independent policy-evaluator validation (highest priority)

**Hypothesis.** A single frozen paired policy-value interval attains its declared coverage throughout a specified design region, while negative controls fail. **Design.** Use fresh simulator seeds and at least two generator families not used to select the interval. Cross contact assignment (`0.5`, `0.2`, `0.1`), event rarity, sample size, batch cap, and clustered customers separately. Include variable `e(X)` only as a *different* overlap experiment. Compare frozen paired DR interval, paired IPW sensitivity, and a design-respecting cluster/batch alternative. **Measures.** Empirical coverage against known `V(pi1)-V(pi0)`, lower Wilson bound for coverage, false launch rate under zero/negative true gain, interval width, ESS, and runtime. Set an explicit simulation budget and multiple-cell acceptance rule before inspecting results; the existing 0.92 lower-bound gate across many cells needs its family-level false-failure behavior calibrated. **Stop.** Narrow the licensed region on failure; do not tune on the same test seeds and call the result confirmation. This addresses the repository's post-hoc interval-selection block before any policy launch claim.

### E2 — Baseline adjustment for scarce Hillstrom events

**Hypothesis.** A prespecified interacted regression adjustment reduces uncertainty for conversion/spend contrasts without changing the randomized estimand. **Design.** On development only, compare unadjusted arm means, Lin-style treatment-by-baseline adjustment using `log1p(history)` and `recency`, and one simple predeclared nonlinear outcome adjustment. Keep arm comparisons and features fixed; avoid searching covariates on the same outcomes. **Measures.** Point estimate, paired/design-based standard error, interval width, arm conversion counts, and training time. For actual coverage, simulate randomized data with known potential outcomes calibrated only from development summaries; rerandomizing a fixed observed outcome table does not reveal individual counterfactuals. **Decision.** Keep adjustment only if the design-valid interval and simulation checks improve precision without misleading coverage. The real-data interval-width change is evidence of precision, not of improved causal truth.

### E3 — When do effect learners beat simple risk targeting?

**Hypothesis.** Gains depend on *events in each arm* and effect heterogeneity, not total row count alone. **Design.** Compare current M01/M02 and M03–M08, plus one X-learner and one R-learner, with identical development folds, preprocessing, capacity, outcome sign, and tuning budget. Factorial synthetic worlds vary event rate, arm imbalance, heterogeneity strength, nuisance misspecification, and harmful-treatment fraction independently. On Hillstrom development, evaluate Mens and Womens contrasts separately; on Lenta, show observational sensitivity rather than a pooled league table. **Primary measures.** Known-truth net-value regret and harmful-contact share in simulation; independent paired policy-value difference with uncertainty on randomized data. Report AP/Brier only for prediction, RATE/Qini as secondary ranking diagnostics ([GRF RATE guide](https://grf-labs.github.io/grf/articles/rate.html)). **Failure criterion.** If a learner improves ranking but not value, or changes winner across arms/slices, report an inconclusive rank set and retain the simpler policy.

### E4 — Learn the constrained policy directly

**Hypothesis.** Optimizing the action rule under contact and money limits can outperform ranking estimated CATE and truncating at a cap. **Design.** In known-truth simulation compare: no contact, risk top-K, effect top-K, net-benefit top-K, an exact small-batch integer optimizer, and a depth-limited policy tree trained on cross-fitted DR scores. Freeze two cost structures: fixed contact cost and response-dependent cost, motivated by [Haupt & Lessmann (2022)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0377221721004732). On Hillstrom, use all three arms only after defining feasible actions and a common conversion/spend objective; offer costs are scenario assumptions, not observed data. **Measures.** True/estimated net value, regret, constraint violations, action mix, tree stability, runtime, and lower-bound gain over no contact. **Stop.** A direct learner does not advance for a small gain whose uncertainty or cost assumptions reverse the decision.

### E5 — Challenge observational matching claims

**Hypothesis.** Good measured-covariate balance can coexist with biased policy ranking when treatment depends on an unobserved customer attribute. **Design.** Build a semi-synthetic study with real-like baseline covariates but explicitly generated treatment, potential outcomes, and a hidden confounder. Compare matching plus S/X learners, propensity-weighted DR, and an oracle randomized assignment. Increase hidden-confounder strength and measure standardized differences on observed features, propensity overlap, CATE error, and true value regret. This is a critical stress test of the *setting* in [Ruf & Handrich (2026)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/14707853261423690), not a claimed reproduction of their unavailable data-generating mechanism. Apply the same diagnostics to Lenta, but with no known truth and no causal validation claim. **Stop.** If policy conclusions change under plausible hidden bias, mark the observational recommendation exploratory.

### E6 — Transport, drift, and abstention

**Hypothesis.** A model can preserve calibration or ranking under covariate shift yet lose policy value when treatment effects shift. **Design.** Train in a baseline synthetic period; test separate covariate shift, outcome-base-rate shift, treatment-effect shift, and cost shift. Compare no refresh, risk recalibration, effect-model retraining, robust/worst-case allocation, and abstention. Where plausible, use the transport assumptions in [Dahabreh et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9066547/) as an explicit checklist, not as permission to combine Hillstrom and Lenta. **Measures.** True regret, harmful contacts, calibration, support diagnostics, launch/abstain frequency, and worst-slice value. **Stop.** Any apparent average benefit with large harmful-slice loss requires a narrower target population or `NO_LAUNCH`.

### E7 — Multi-action and longitudinal extension (partner-data dependent)

Define three actions first: no contact, low-cost message, high-cost incentive. Require immutable assignment probabilities, action delivery, eligibility, cost, and outcome maturation per customer and time. In a randomized pilot, evaluate a multi-action policy with independent off-policy assessment using the framework of [Zhou et al.](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.2022.2271). Only after genuine repeated histories exist should the project test cooldown, fatigue, competing contacts, and sequential off-policy estimators such as [Kallus & Uehara](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/kallus20b.html). If contacts can spill over within households or networks, cluster design is needed to study interference ([Hudgens & Halloran](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19081744/)). Current public data cannot validate these extensions.

## 5. Product questions derived from the methods

The next planner version should make the research distinction visible to a first-time analyst. Display each proposed action with **why selected**, expected scenario value range, capacity/cost consumption, eligibility reason, and a named no-action baseline. Show a source badge and evidence date beside every metric. Add a sensitivity chart over assumed save probability and offer cost; the point where the recommended action flips is more useful than a single invented ROI. Export the full candidate pool, policy recipe, assumptions, evidence tier, selected action, and rejection reason so another analyst can reproduce the decision. No current public-data screen should display a customer-specific causal uplift or measured profit claim.

For a partner pilot, add an experiment setup flow: action definitions, fixed assignment fractions, holdout size/power, contact exclusions, budget, outcome horizon, and stop conditions. Require a reviewable campaign manifest before assigning actions, record actual delivery and spend, and preserve an untouched control group. The product's most credible differentiator is the ability to say **“insufficient evidence; do not launch”** with the exact failing gate.

## 6. Recommended sequence and portfolio deliverables

| Order | Deliverable | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|
| 1 | Frozen evaluator specification and E1 independent simulation | New seed/generator hashes, coverage and false-promotion table, clear licensed region, negative controls. |
| 2 | E2 adjustment and E3 learner comparison on development roles | Same-fold comparison with event counts, policy-value differences, uncertainty, and honest inconclusive outcomes. |
| 3 | E4 constrained decision benchmark and E5 hidden-confounding stress | Known-truth regret/violations and a documented observational sensitivity limit. |
| 4 | E6 shift/abstention study; planner sensitivity and evidence UX | Drift report, no-launch decisions, reproducible evidence export, five first-user task observations. |
| 5 | Registered confirmation or partner pilot, if earlier gates pass | A single locked primary contrast and validated interval; recorded assignment, costs, and mature outcomes. |

For **Data Scientist** roles, lead with estimands, randomized design, event counts, uncertainty, and a negative result. For **ML Engineer** roles, lead with reproducible data/model builds, typed action/evidence contracts, serving parity, and failure-closed gates. For **AI Engineer** roles, a grounded report assistant is a later optional feature: measure source accuracy and refusal on claims the evidence cannot support before adding generation. The technical contribution is a defensible, end-to-end decision process with explicit boundaries; do not claim invention of the underlying causal estimators.
